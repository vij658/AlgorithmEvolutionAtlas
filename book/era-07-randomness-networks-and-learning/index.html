The Algorithm Evolution Atlas · Era 7 · 1979 to 1995

Randomness, Networks and Learning

Thirteen steps, from Khachiyan's polynomial-time linear programming of 1979 to AdaBoost in 1995. Along the way, one short hash vouches for a mountain of data, computers agree despite traitors, a search cools like metal, a tree rearranges itself as it is used, a proof convinces without revealing, networks of simple units learn, stars are grouped by a tree of cubes, text is searched by fingerprints and sorted by its rotations, the Internet learns to share its links, and a quantum computer is shown to factor quickly, on paper.

Field note from the visiting historian. In the sixth era, the species learned that some problems may have no fast method. In this one, it learns to use chance on purpose, to keep networks of computers working when parts fail or lie, and to make machines that learn from examples.
